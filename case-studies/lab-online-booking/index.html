Lab Online Booking: one system, an entire province

Lab Online Booking (LOB) is the outpatient laboratory scheduling service TSB Healthcare has operated for British Columbia since 2018. It runs on Slot IQ™. Patients book lab visits at 69 sites run by four separate health providers — regional health authorities and a faith-based provider, each with its own management, staff and budget — all on one platform: one owner-level view over many sites that operate very differently.

The data

What the province-wide data shows

  • Site data finds hidden capacity. No-show rates ranged from under 1% at the best sites to over 12% at the worst. Bringing just two high-volume sites to their network average would recover about 8,500 slots a year.
  • Self-service varies, and that’s the lever. The share of bookings made online ranged from about 59% to 99% by provider. Every point gained moves phone work off the front desk.
  • Owners get one comparable view. Volume, utilization, no-shows and cancellations are reported the same way at every site, so leaders can benchmark sites and act on the outliers.

* Staff-time estimates assume about 5 minutes of staff phone time per online booking at $35/hour. Figures come from Lab Online Booking platform reporting (data through November 2025) and are rounded. Health providers are not named to respect client confidentiality.

The return

Productivity and staff time saved, year after year

Every booking a patient makes online is a phone call a staff member doesn’t take. Across the platform, that shift has grown every year, and the savings compound as volume grows.

Rule of thumb for a portfolio: every 10,000 bookings moved online frees about 830 staff hours, roughly $29,000 in front-desk time, before counting slots recovered from cancellations and no-shows.

What changed

  • Phone work moved online. About 103,000 bookings were made by patients themselves instead of through a staff phone call.
  • Adoption kept climbing. Volume grew from about 5,600 appointments in the first year to about 35,800 in 2025.
  • Load stayed balanced. Both sites ran near full, with neither sitting idle while the other was overloaded.

What the data revealed

  • A hidden workflow gap. The network’s no-show rate (8.9%) was above the platform average (6.6%). Reporting traced part of it to one site not recording late arrivals as arrived.
  • Peak no-show windows. Most missed appointments clustered between noon and 3 pm — a specific window for reminders and overbooking rules.
  • Strong retention. 96% of the network’s patients booked only at its own sites.
Across all four health providers

Patients cross boundaries. Most systems can’t see it.

Because Slot IQ runs across four BC health providers at once, it sees what no single provider’s system can: patients who book wherever there is an open slot — the behaviour a multi-site network wants between its own clinics.

The takeaway: patients who are shown open times at more than one site will use them. These patients also cancelled more often (24.8% vs. 14.0%) because some book backup slots. Reminders and easy self-cancellation return those slots to the schedule instead of leaving them empty — and Slot IQ Flow offers them to the next waiting patient.
