Kyruus is a strong fit when…

  • A consumer-grade find-a-doctor experience across a large provider network is the priority — that is Kyruus’s core product and it leads the category.
  • Provider data governance matters: keeping specialties, locations, insurance participation and availability accurate across many systems.
  • You are a health plan building member-facing provider directories and matching.

TSB is a stronger fit when…

  • The problem starts after the patient chooses a provider: booking the visit, arriving, queuing and being seen on time.
  • Volume runs through labs, imaging and outpatient clinics, where walk-ins mix with booked patients and patient flow management decides the day.
  • You want patient scheduling, intake, kiosks, queuing and flow analytics from one platform.
  • Procurement requires support for PIPEDA and FIPPA obligations, or Canadian data residency.
Switching

What changing the booking layer involves

Kyruus and TSB overlap mainly on patient scheduling, so a switch is usually about the booking layer, not the whole access stack. The provider directory can stay. Here is what the move actually looks like.

Decide what is moving

Separate search from scheduling. If the find-a-doctor experience works, keep it and point its booking buttons at TSB. If you are consolidating, plan the directory replacement as its own workstream — it involves provider data, not appointments.

Appointment data stays put

Bookings made through any patient-facing layer are written to your EHR or scheduling system, and that system remains the source of truth. TSB connects to the same records, so upcoming appointments continue unchanged and history is preserved. There is no bulk appointment migration to run.

Re-integrate the EHR

The real work is mapping appointment types, visit durations, locations and provider schedules into TSB’s booking rules — including modality-specific rules for imaging and draw-station logic for labs that a general directory never needed. Budget most of the timeline here and test with real schedules.

Run both front ends in parallel

Old booking links keep working while new entry points roll out per site or per service line. The most common mistake is forgetting scattered entry points — campaign landing pages, Google Business Profile links and call-center scripts that still route to the old flow. Inventory them early and switch them deliberately.

Then extend beyond booking

Once scheduling is live, the parts a directory never covered come next: self check-in, one fair queue for walk-ins and booked patients, and flow analytics for hospital and health-system operations teams. Staff retraining is light at the front desk and heaviest for access-center agents, who gain live availability across every location.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is TSB Healthcare a good Kyruus alternative?
Yes — especially if you want the whole patient access and flow journey in one platform. TSB adds self check-in kiosks, a queue engine and a virtual waiting room, and real-time slot optimization on top of online booking and access analytics.
What does TSB do that Kyruus focuses less on?
In-clinic flow: self check-in, blending walk-ins and booked patients into one fair queue, now-serving displays, and automatically re-filling cancelled slots to recover capacity.
Does TSB cover provider matching?
TSB focuses on access, scheduling and flow rather than provider search. It integrates with your systems and is a strong fit for high-volume outpatient, diagnostics and imaging access.
Do we have to choose between Kyruus and TSB?
Not necessarily. The two products solve adjacent problems. Kyruus helps patients find the right provider; TSB manages what happens after that choice, from booking through check-in to being seen. Some organisations keep a provider directory in place and add TSB as the scheduling and flow layer behind it.
What happens to our existing appointment data if we switch the booking layer?
Appointments booked through a directory or access center live in your EHR or scheduling system, and that system stays the source of truth. TSB connects to the same records, so future appointments continue unchanged and no booking history is lost when the patient-facing layer changes.
How long does a TSB implementation take for a health system?
It scales with the number of sites and integrations. A single outpatient department or lab can go live quickly once EHR access is arranged, while a health-system rollout is phased site by site. Running the old and new booking front ends in parallel during the transition is normal, so there is no hard cutover date to hit.
