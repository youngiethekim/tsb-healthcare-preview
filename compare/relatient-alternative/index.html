Relatient is a strong fit when…

  • You are a U.S. physician group or specialty practice that wants scheduling, reminders and payments tied tightly to your existing system of record.
  • Messaging is the core need: recall campaigns, no-show reduction and two-way texting at group scale.
  • Collecting patient balances and payment messaging matter as much as scheduling itself.

TSB is a stronger fit when…

  • Walk-ins are a fact of life — labs, imaging and outpatient clinics where booked and unbooked patients share one waiting room.
  • You need the on-site layer: kiosks, a virtual waiting room, now-serving displays and patient flow management, not just messages before the visit.
  • You want patient scheduling, intake, queuing and analytics as one platform from booking to being seen.
  • Procurement requires support for PIPEDA and FIPPA obligations, or Canadian data residency.
Switching

What moving off Relatient actually involves

Because engagement platforms sit on top of your EHR rather than replacing it, a switch is a front-end change, not a data migration. Appointments, patient records and schedules stay exactly where they are. The work is in four places.

Rebuild the scheduling rules

Self-scheduling is only as good as its rules: which appointment types each provider takes, visit durations, locations, buffers and exceptions. These rules are the part teams most often underestimate, because they were tuned over years in the old system. Export them, prune the ones nobody remembers creating, and rebuild the survivors in TSB against real calendars before any patient sees the new booking flow.

Cut reminders over cleanly

Run the migration one site or service line at a time and make sure each visit is owned by exactly one system, so no patient gets reminders from both. Keep message content consistent through the switch — patients notice a change in tone more than a change in vendor.

Reconnect the integration

TSB connects to the same EHR interfaces Relatient used. Integration testing is the long pole: patient matching, appointment types, locations and provider schedules all need verifying against live data, and diagnostics settings add modality and draw-station rules a practice-focused tool never carried.

Add what was never there

The step change comes after cutover, when arrivals stop being invisible. Self check-in feeds one fair queue for booked and walk-in patients, displays call patients forward, and flow analytics show where the waits actually happen. Front-desk retraining is short; the bigger shift is operational, as supervisors start managing the day from live data. What typically goes wrong — double reminders, stale booking links on your website, forgotten scheduling rules — is predictable and avoidable with a checklist.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is TSB Healthcare a good Relatient alternative?
Yes. TSB matches Relatient on self-scheduling, SMS and check-in, and adds a virtual waiting room, a queue engine that blends walk-ins with booked patients, and flow analytics — a strong fit for diagnostics and high-volume outpatient.
What does TSB add beyond scheduling and messaging?
The in-clinic flow layer: one fair queue for walk-in and booked patients, now-serving displays, real-time slot optimization and operational analytics that turn flow into recovered capacity.
Does it handle walk-ins?
Yes — TSB blends booked and walk-in patients into one intelligent queue, which is especially valuable for labs, imaging and outpatient services.
Can we run TSB and Relatient in parallel during a migration?
Yes. The usual pattern is to move one site or one service line at a time, so reminders and self-scheduling keep working everywhere while each location switches. The main thing to manage is message deduplication, so patients never receive reminders from both systems for the same visit.
What happens to our existing appointment and reminder data?
Your appointments live in your EHR, and they stay there. TSB reads the same schedule through its integration, so upcoming visits and reminder workflows continue without a manual migration. Historical message logs stay with the outgoing vendor, so export anything you need for reference before the contract ends.
How long does switching from Relatient to TSB take?
A single practice moving scheduling, reminders and check-in typically goes live in weeks once EHR integration is in place. Larger groups phase the rollout by location. Budget extra time for rebuilding scheduling rules, because provider and appointment-type rules are the part teams most often underestimate.
