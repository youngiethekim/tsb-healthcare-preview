Phreesia is a strong fit when…

  • Intake, consents, eligibility checks and point-of-service payments are the problem you are solving — that is Phreesia’s centre of gravity, and it is deep.
  • You run a U.S. ambulatory practice on a common EHR and want pre-built intake integration with payment collection.
  • Screening questionnaires, patient-reported outcomes and payment plans matter more than waiting-room operations.

TSB is a stronger fit when…

  • The bottleneck starts after check-in: waits, queues and room flow across labs, imaging and outpatient clinics.
  • You need walk-ins and booked patients blended into one fair queue, with kiosks, displays and patient flow management built in.
  • You want patient scheduling, intake, queuing and analytics from one healthcare-native platform instead of an intake point solution.
  • Procurement requires support for PIPEDA and FIPPA obligations, or Canadian data residency.
Switching

What moving off Phreesia actually involves

Replacing an intake platform is less disruptive than replacing an EHR, because the system of record does not move. Your appointments, patient records and completed intake documents live in the EHR, and they stay there. What changes is the patient-facing layer on top. A typical switch to TSB runs through five steps.

1. Map what you actually use

Most intake deployments accumulate form templates over the years. Before anything is rebuilt, audit which forms, consents and questionnaires are still in active use and migrate only those. This is usually the single biggest time-saver in the whole project.

2. Reconnect the EHR

TSB integrates with the same scheduling and demographic interfaces your intake vendor used, so appointment data flows through the integration rather than a manual export. Integration testing — matching patients, appointment types and locations — is the step to schedule generously, especially for hospitals and health systems with more than one EHR instance.

3. Run both in parallel

Teams rarely cut over in one day. A common pattern keeps Phreesia in place for payments while TSB takes over check-in, queuing and flow; another switches complete sites one at a time. Either way, suppress duplicate reminders so patients hear from only one system per visit.

4. Retrain the front desk

Front-desk staff pick up kiosk-assisted check-in and the queue dashboard in a short session, because the new workflow removes steps rather than adding them. The bigger change is for supervisors, who gain live wait-time and flow analytics they did not have before.

5. Decommission deliberately

Export any reporting history you want to keep before the old contract ends, confirm the final billing date, and only then retire the old tablets and signage. What typically goes wrong in a switch is not data loss — it is double messaging during parallel running and underestimated form cleanup. Both are avoidable with a plan.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is TSB Healthcare a good Phreesia alternative?
Yes — especially if your priority is patient flow. TSB pairs self check-in with a virtual waiting room, queue management, online booking, an AI assistant and flow analytics in one platform.
Does TSB handle walk-ins as well as booked patients?
Yes. TSB blends booked and walk-in patients into one fair queue with self check-in — a strong fit for labs, imaging and high-volume outpatient settings.
What does TSB add beyond check-in?
A virtual waiting room and queue engine, cross-location online booking, a 24/7 AI assistant, automatic cancellation fill, and analytics that turn flow into recovered capacity.
Can we run TSB and Phreesia in parallel during a migration?
Yes, and many teams do. A common pattern keeps Phreesia in place for intake and payments while TSB takes over queuing, check-in and patient flow, with a full cutover decided later. Multi-site organisations usually switch site by site instead, so no location is ever without a working front desk.
What happens to our existing appointment and intake data?
Booked appointments live in your EHR, not in the intake vendor, so they carry forward through the EHR integration rather than a manual export. Completed intake forms that Phreesia has already written back to your EHR stay where they are. TSB starts capturing check-in and flow data from the first day it goes live.
How long does a switch from Phreesia to TSB take?
It depends on scope. A single clinic replacing check-in and adding queuing typically goes live in weeks. A multi-site rollout with EHR integration and custom intake forms is phased over a longer period, one site at a time. The slowest steps are usually integration testing and deciding which intake forms are still needed.
