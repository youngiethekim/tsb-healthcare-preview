The short answer

What is patient flow management software?

Patient flow management software tracks and coordinates every patient visit from arrival to completion — check-in, queue position, service and discharge — giving staff a live operational picture and leaders the data to fix the bottlenecks that cost capacity.

It follows the visit, not just the booking

A schedule ends the moment a patient walks in. Patient flow software picks the visit up there and tracks state — arrived, waiting, in service, complete — so nobody is lost between the front desk and the treatment room.

It makes the wait visible to everyone

Staff see the real queue instead of a lobby full of faces. Patients see where they stand on displays and on their phone. Uncertainty is most of what makes a wait feel long.

It shows where the day breaks down

Late starts, over-running appointment types, a room that sits empty every Tuesday afternoon — flow data names the specific bottleneck instead of describing a general sense of busyness.

It turns delay into recoverable capacity

Every minute of avoidable wait is a minute of clinical capacity you already paid for. Flow analytics quantifies it, so improvement work has a number attached.

Real-time visibility

From arrival to completion, in one live view

TSB gives front-line staff and operations leaders the same picture of the day — who has arrived, who is waiting, how long they have waited, and what is free right now.

  • Live queue status for every service line, updated as patients check in and are called.
  • Now-serving displays and phone updates so patients know exactly where they stand.
  • Wait and service timers on every visit, so delays surface while they can still be fixed.

Where patient flow breaks down

Four bottlenecks that quietly cost you the day

The front desk is the bottleneck

Every arrival queues for the same two staff members. Check-in time stretches, and the clinical schedule starts late because patients reach the room late. Self check-in removes the queue before it forms.

Nobody knows the real wait

Without live status, staff answer “how much longer?” by guessing, and patients ask again five minutes later. Both cost staff time that never appears in any report.

Empty slots go unnoticed

A cancellation at 09:40 is invisible by 10:00. Without flow data, idle capacity is only discovered at month end — if at all.

Sites are managed in isolation

One clinic is overwhelmed while another two miles away runs at half capacity, because no one view spans both. Network-level flow makes that imbalance obvious and actionable.

How it works

Four moving parts, one connected system

Patient flow management only works when arrival, queue, service and measurement share the same data. TSB ships them as one platform rather than four integrations.

1. Arrival & intake

Patients check in at a kiosk, on their phone or at the desk, and complete intake for their visit type before they sit down.

2. Queue & routing

The visit enters the right queue with the right priority, and displays keep both staff and patients informed of position and progress.

3. Service & completion

Rooms, providers and equipment are matched to the visit; completion closes the loop and frees the resource for the next patient.

4. Measurement

Every state change is timestamped, so wait, service and idle capacity are reported rather than estimated.

Scope

Where TSB sits alongside inpatient systems

“Patient flow” means different things either side of admission, and being precise about it matters when you are comparing tools.

The outpatient journey, not bed management

Inpatient platforms handle bed management and length of stay for admitted patients. TSB manages the ambulatory patient journey — arrival, queue, service, completion. The two solve adjacent problems, not the same one.

One real-time view of patient status

Front-line staff and operations leaders share a single real-time view of patient status across every site: who has arrived, who is waiting, who is in service and who is finished.

A cleaner handover between steps

When a patient moves from reception to a treatment room to a follow-on service, the handover carries their status with them — so nobody restarts the visit or loses track of where a patient sits.

Supports patient safety, without surveillance

Knowing where every patient sits in a visit supports patient safety: nobody is forgotten in a corridor. Clinicians see the operational status they need — not clinical detail a flow system has no business holding.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is patient flow management software?
Patient flow management software is the system that tracks and coordinates a patient’s journey through a site — arrival, check-in, waiting, service and completion — and gives staff a live view of where everyone is and what is holding the day up. Unlike a scheduling calendar, which decides when appointments happen, a patient flow management system manages what happens once patients start arriving: queue order, room and resource readiness, delays, and the capacity that is free right now.
How is patient flow software different from scheduling software?
Scheduling software builds the day; patient flow software runs it. Scheduling decides appointment types, durations, provider matching and how slots are offered. Patient flow takes over at arrival — check-in, queue position, called-next displays, service time and completion. TSB provides both in one platform, so the schedule you planned and the day you actually run are measured against each other instead of living in separate systems.
Does it work across multiple hospitals and clinics?
Yes. TSB runs patient flow across 69 sites from one platform. Each location keeps its own queues, appointment types and service rules, while regional and executive leaders get a single live view across the network — which sites are backing up, where capacity is idle, and how today compares with the same day last month.
Is a patient flow management system the same as patient tracking in a hospital?
They overlap. Hospital patient tracking usually means knowing where a patient physically is. Patient flow management includes that, but adds the operational layer around it: why a patient is waiting, what the queue will look like in twenty minutes, which rooms and staff are free, and what the delay is costing in throughput. TSB tracks visit state rather than individuals — the flow view is built on appointment and queue status, not staff location monitoring.
What results do health systems see from patient flow software?
Across TSB deployments the measured outcomes include a 70% reduction in check-in time, ~188K staff hours saved, 112K+ appointments recovered through fewer no-shows, and modeled $26M+ in operational cost savings — across 4M+ appointments. Your own numbers depend on volume and starting wait times; the ROI calculator estimates them from your site counts.
Does patient flow management software integrate with our EHR?
Yes. TSB is built to connect with your EHR, EMR and LIS so arrivals, appointment status and completions stay in sync with the systems your staff already work in. Flow data flows back as well, so wait time and throughput reporting reflects the same source of truth clinicians use.
