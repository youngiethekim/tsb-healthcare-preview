First, the distinction that matters

Patient scheduling, not nurse rostering

“Hospital scheduling software” means two different products, and vendors rarely say which one they are. TSB is the patient one. It does not plan shifts, build nurse rosters or manage workforce hours — it schedules patient appointments and manages patient flow through the hospital.

What it schedules: patients

Outpatient appointments, imaging and lab bookings, follow-ups and recalls — the demand side of the hospital. Every template describes bookable patient capacity, not an employee’s working hours.

What it deliberately is not

No shift patterns, no rostering rules, no leave management. Hospitals keep their workforce system; TSB sits beside it and keeps the clinics that rostered staff run full of booked patients.

What it adds around the appointment

Booking is a third of the job. The same platform handles reminders, self check-in, queue management and flow tracking, so the appointment it books is also the visit it runs.

Why the distinction pays

A patient scheduling platform is judged on filled slots, shorter waits and fewer no-shows — hospital capacity metrics a rostering tool was never built to move.

Across the hospital

Appointment scheduling for every department and service line

An MRI list, a fracture clinic and a phlebotomy queue book nothing alike. TSB gives each department its own appointment types, durations, resources and rules — on one platform, so the hospital finally has a single picture of its bookable capacity.

  • Department-level templates for clinics, imaging, labs and outpatient services, each with its own rules.
  • Resource-aware booking that reserves the scanner, the room or the bay along with the slot.
  • One appointment record from referral or request through booking, arrival and completion.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is this hospital scheduling software for nursing shifts or staff rosters?
No, and it is worth being direct about: TSB does not do nurse rostering, shift planning or workforce management. TSB is hospital scheduling software for patients — it books and manages patient appointments and runs patient flow from arrival to completion. If you are searching for staff scheduling, you need a workforce tool; if you need patients booked, reminded, checked in and moved through the building, that is what this platform does.
What is hospital scheduling software?
In the patient sense, hospital scheduling software is the system that manages appointment capacity across a hospital’s outpatient departments, clinics, imaging and labs — the templates that define each service’s bookable time, the booking channels patients use, and the reminders, check-in and queue management that carry the appointment through the day. It is the operational front door of the hospital, sitting alongside the EHR rather than replacing it.
Can one hospital appointment management system cover every department?
Yes, and that is the point of doing it centrally. Each department keeps its own appointment types, durations, resources and rules — an MRI slot behaves nothing like a fracture clinic follow-up — but they run on one platform. Patients get one way to book and one style of reminder, and the hospital gets one view of capacity instead of a different silo per service line.
Does the hospital online booking system work across multiple sites?
Yes. TSB runs scheduling across 69 sites today. A health system can offer patients the nearest available slot across every eligible location, move demand from an overloaded site to a quiet one, and give regional leaders one live picture of cross-site capacity — while each site keeps local control of its own templates and rules.
Does hospital scheduling software integrate with the EHR?
Yes. TSB is built to connect with your EHR, EMR and LIS so appointments, demographics, arrivals and completions stay in sync with the clinical record. Booking staff work in the scheduler, clinicians stay in the chart, and reporting draws on one source of truth rather than reconciled extracts.
How does scheduling connect to patient flow on the day?
The appointment is only the plan; the day is what actually happens. Because TSB also runs check-in, queueing and flow tracking, the schedule and the day are the same record — a late-running clinic is visible as it happens, a no-show frees a slot that can be refilled, and wait times are measured against the plan. Across deployments that closed loop has recovered 112K+ appointments.
