Redesign prototype · patient booking

LabOnlineBooking.ca, rebuilt around the patient's task

A clickable prototype of the booking flow. Try it: book a test, or use “Change or cancel” to look up a sample appointment. The design notes are under the prototype.

Prototype: locations, times and the booking code are sample data. Nothing is sent anywhere.
    Design notes

    What changed and why

    Most people who open a lab booking site are holding a paper requisition, often on a phone, sometimes for a parent. They want one thing: a time at a lab that can do their test. Each change below removes a step, a guess, or a dead end on the way to that.

    01

    Three clear doors on the home screen

    Problem: booking, changing a booking and finding a lab compete with notices and long text.

    Change: one primary button (Book a lab test) and two secondary ones. Notices shrink to a short “Before you book” checklist.

    02

    Ask for the test first

    Problem: picking a location first lets people book a site that can't do their test, and they find out at the counter.

    Change: the test choice filters locations, so every lab shown can do it. “I'm not sure” is a valid answer.

    03

    Show the next opening on every location

    Problem: patients open location after location hunting for a time.

    Change: each card shows its next available time, distance, health authority and access features. Sort by nearest or earliest.

    04

    An “earliest time” shortcut

    Problem: calendars make people scan day by day.

    Change: a one-tap earliest slot, a date strip with open-slot counts, and times grouped into morning, afternoon and evening.

    05

    Fewer, plainer form fields

    Problem: long forms with unclear formats cause errors and abandoned bookings.

    Change: only what the lab needs. Each field says where to find the value (the PHN is on the BC Services Card). Errors appear next to the field and in a summary at the top.

    06

    “Your visit” summary that stays in view

    Problem: by step four people forget what they picked and press Back, losing their place.

    Change: a summary panel (a collapsible bar on phones) with a Change link for each choice. The review page works the same way.

    07

    Change or cancel without an account

    Problem: people who can't find a self-serve change usually phone the lab or don't show up.

    Change: look up a booking with the code and date of birth, then reschedule or cancel with an on-page confirmation.

    08

    Built for older eyes and other languages

    Problem: many lab patients are seniors or read English as a second language.

    Change: a Larger text toggle, a language menu in the header, 48px touch targets, visible focus, real form labels, and a dark mode that follows the device.

    09

    Say where LifeLabs is booked

    Problem: patients mix up the two BC networks and search here for a LifeLabs centre.

    Change: one line on the home screen and in empty search results points them to LifeLabs' own site.

    10

    A confirmation page that answers “now what?”

    Problem: a bare confirmation number leaves people unsure what to bring or how to prepare.

    Change: the code, what to bring, fasting instructions for that test, directions, and a manage-booking link on one screen.
