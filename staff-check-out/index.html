Three states, one screen

Checked in → called → completed

Every patient in the building sits in exactly one of three states, and moving between them takes a single tap. That is the whole interface, and it is deliberate — a station that gets used two hundred times a day cannot afford a learning curve.

  • Call the next patient — the code goes to the waiting room display and out by SMS in the same action.
  • Close out the visit — mark it completed when the patient leaves, and the slot frees for the queue engine there and then.
  • Mark arrived, no-show or cancelled — on booked appointments, so the day’s record matches the day.
  • Refreshes itself — the arrivals list and walk-in queue update live, so two staff working the same queue never call the same person.
Why timings stop being guesses

Recorded as it happens, not reconstructed at month end

Most wait-time reporting is an estimate assembled weeks later from whatever people remembered to write down. When arrival, call and completion are each a tap someone was going to make anyway, the timings are simply a by-product of the shift.

Real wait, per patient

Time from check-in to called, and called to completed, for every visit — which is what an actual wait time is, as opposed to what the schedule said should have happened.

Feeds the analytics directly

Every state change is an event the flow analytics read. No separate audit exercise, no one keying a tally sheet into a spreadsheet on Friday.

Attributable, by design

Actions are logged with who took them and what changed, before and after. That is an audit requirement for health authorities — and the fastest way to answer “what actually happened with this patient?”

Frequently asked questions

What is the staff check-out station?
It is the staff-facing screen behind the counter or at the point of care, where your team calls the next patient from the queue, marks them as seen, and closes out the visit. It is the counterpart to the patient-facing check-in kiosk: the kiosk opens a visit, the station finishes it.
How is it different from the check-in kiosk?
The check-in kiosk is for patients and sits in the lobby: it takes the arrival and issues a ticket. The check-out station is for staff and sits behind the desk or in the room: it calls patients, completes visits and handles anything self-service could not. Both read and write the same queue.
What hardware does it run on?
It runs in a browser, so it works on the workstation already on the desk, a tablet carried into the collection room, or a second screen at a call centre. Sites commonly run one at reception and one at each point of care.
Can two staff work the same queue at once?
Yes. The arrivals list and walk-in queue refresh live, and calling a patient moves them out of the waiting state immediately, so two people working side by side do not call the same patient twice.
Is every action recorded?
Yes. Staff actions are audit-logged with the values before and after the change, and access is role-based — receptionist, lab staff, call centre and administrator each get only what their role requires. Login history is retained. See security and compliance.
Does it work across multiple sites?
Yes. Staff can switch between the locations they are authorised for, and administrators see across an organization — which is how health authorities run dozens of collection sites without a separate system per building.
